## 1. Anthropic prices IPO at $83, raising $81B to become largest debut ever

*CNBC · 8 min read · Lead*

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share on Wednesday, well above its $70–$78 target range, raising $81.3 billion in the largest stock-market debut on record and eclipsing SpaceX's $85.7 billion June offering. The upsized deal of 980 million shares (up from 825 million) values the company at approximately $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis and $1.1 trillion non-diluted. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley led a 22-bank syndicate; the stock begins trading Thursday on Nasdaq under ticker ANT.

The pricing caps a roadshow that drew roughly $400 billion in institutional indications of interest and nearly $100 billion from retail investors, according to CNBC sources. Amazon and Google, both existing backers with stakes valued over $100 billion each, joined a group of tech companies including Microsoft, Nvidia, and AMD in committing $26 billion to the private placement at the IPO price. Anthropic's nine-month revenue through September hit $33.24 billion, up roughly 1,200% year over year, with a run-rate of $81 billion as of October 31. The company reported a $104 billion net loss for the nine months, driven largely by fair-value changes on convertible notes.

For all the demand, the filing lays bare the capital intensity of frontier AI. Anthropic spent nearly $9 billion on compute in the first half of 2026 — nearly 4x the prior year — and has committed nearly $900 billion in future compute spending with partners including Amazon and Google. Its prospectus warns that some infrastructure partners depend on third-party financing; if that financing becomes unavailable or costlier, contracted capacity could be delayed or reduced. The company also faces intensifying competition: OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra captured about 13% of enterprise AI spend tracked by Ramp versus 8% for Anthropic's Claude Fable, and 59% of large-company executives plan to increase use of open-weight models to rein in costs. Meta, one of Anthropic's largest customers, is developing internal capabilities to reduce reliance on external models.

CEO Dario Amodei has called for the industry to slow the pace of releasing increasingly powerful models, warning that advances could outstrip society's ability to manage risks. The prospectus flagged that models could develop capabilities faster than the company can evaluate and safeguard them. Anthropic is considering tighter human oversight of some R&D decisions as models take on a growing share of that work.

**Verdict:** Read in full — the largest IPO ever, with candid risk disclosures on compute dependence, competition from open weights, and the safety-commercial tension that defines frontier AI. [Read the original](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html)

### 7. Java4Shell: Critical RCE in OpenJDK Base64 decoder (CVE-2026-114938)

*Calif.io · 6 min read · Discovery*

Security firm Calif disclosed Java4Shell, a memory-corruption vulnerability in OpenJDK's Base64 decoder scoring CVSS 4.0 9.2 (Critical). A malformed Base64 input of just 64 'A' characters followed by '=' triggers an out-of-bounds write in hand-rolled AVX-512 VBMI assembly, enabling pre-auth RCE in Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, Artifactory, Jenkins, PingFederate, and post-auth RCE in Kafka, Solr, Elasticsearch, Gerrit, Spinnaker. OpenJDK 17.0.6+ and all downstream distributions (Amazon Corretto, Eclipse Temurin, Microsoft Build, Azul Zulu, BellSoft Liberica, SAP SapMachine, Red Hat, Oracle) are affected on x86-64 CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI (Intel Ice Lake+, AMD Zen 4+). ARM64 is not affected.

Calif used AI-assisted discovery ("centaur hacker" approach): researcher Quang Luong found the bug in one hour and built the first exploit in two more hours at under $200 token cost. The write-up argues for "vibe-uncoding" — using AI to identify and remove dead code and needless optimizations that expand attack surface. Mitigations: update immediately (OpenJDK and Oracle patches released Nov 17), or start with `-XX:-UseBASE64Intrinsics` or `-XX:UseAVX=2`.

**Verdict:** Read in full — the first critical memory-corruption bug in Java's "safe" runtime in years, affecting the backbone of enterprise infrastructure; the AI-assisted discovery method signals a shift in vulnerability research economics. [Read the original](https://calif.io/java4shell/)

### 8. China Telecom trains 285B-parameter Xing4 MoE entirely on 8,192 Huawei Ascend 910C chips

*Hugging Face / arXiv · 5 min read · Discovery*

China Telecom's Xingchen AGI Lab (138 authors) posted a technical report detailing Xing4, a frontier agentic LLM series trained entirely on domestic hardware: 8,192 Ascend 910C NPUs using the MindSpore HyperParallel stack — no GPUs, no CUDA. The flagship 285B-A17B model (285B total, 17B active) and open-weight 29B-A4B variant (29B total, 4B active, 256K–1M context, Apache 2.0) integrate Multi-head Latent Attention, DeepSeek Sparse Attention, IndexShare, and DSpark speculative decoding. Reported gains: 4.68x prefill throughput over MLA baseline, 1.35x step-time speedup by removing pipeline parallelism, 2.67–3.77x decoding throughput from DSpark. The 29B model scores 75.0 on SWE-bench Verified (vs 53.0 Gemma4-26B-A4B, 76.0 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B) and 57.5 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (vs 30.0, 51.5) — developer-reported, not independently verified.

The report reads as an engineering log of co-designing architecture, memory, communication, and host scheduling for Ascend clusters. CPU affinity and core isolation reduce host scheduling interference; topology-aware hybrid sharding and coordinated long-sequence memory management enable the 285B run. TeleGym, a unified agent execution platform, connects execution, evaluation, and trajectory construction to policy optimization with asynchronous execution and partial rollouts.

**Verdict:** Read in full — the most detailed public account of training a frontier-scale MoE on a non-CUDA stack; if independently replicated, it provides an engineering blueprint for organizations seeking alternatives to Western accelerator ecosystems. [Read the original](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22115)
